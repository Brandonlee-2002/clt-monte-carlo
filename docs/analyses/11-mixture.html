1 Population and prediction

This analysis uses the custom age-at-death distribution in Inference/custom_age_at_death_distribution_monte_carlo.html. The reference code defines integer ages from 0 through 100, adds an early-life exponential weight and an adult bell-shaped weight centered at age 65, and normalizes the combined weights so they sum to one. The project implements that same generator through custom_age_at_death_distribution() and rdeath() in R/helpers.R.

This is a synthetic teaching distribution designed to reproduce the supplied shape; it is not a fitted historical mortality table. Because it has finite mean and variance, the CLT predicts eventual convergence of sample means, but the two-component shape should make small-sample normality less immediate than for symmetric populations.

The distribution has theoretical mean 49.98 years and standard deviation 27.63 years. It assigns approximately 24.37% of its probability to ages below 20 and 53.29% to ages 60 and older. These values are calculated from the normalized discrete probabilities, not estimated from the Monte Carlo sample means.

3 Experimental design and decision rule

I generated B = 10000 sample means at candidate sizes 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 300, 500. The common criteria are absolute skewness ≤ 0.20, absolute excess kurtosis ≤ 0.30, Q-Q correlation ≥ 0.995, and standardized Q-Q RMSE ≤ 0.08. Three consecutive passing values are required.

6 Conclusion

The first stable pass is n = 30 on the project’s candidate grid. The separate exhaustive scan of every integer from 2 through 500 reports n = 17. These values answer slightly different questions: the candidate-grid result is the first stable pass among the published summary sizes, while the exhaustive result refines the boundary to consecutive integers.

The early-life decline and adult peak create a non-normal population shape that averaging must smooth. The result is evidence for this synthetic death distribution under the stated criteria—not a universal threshold for real mortality data.
